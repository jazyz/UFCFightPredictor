Method

No peeking: walk-forward from the day before the window

Every prediction comes from a model that had never seen the fight — or anything after it. The ensemble trains on fights before 2025-08-30, then retrains twice as the year advances (2026-02-28 and 2026-08-29, the pipeline's 182-day cadence). Hyperparameters are frozen from the July 2026 search; nothing in this window was used to tune anything. This report runs on the current pipeline — the one deployed today, including the height/reach and stance features.

Coverage: the year had 547 fights with recorded results. The model scored 282 of them. It skipped 258 fights it cannot predict by design — women's bouts are excluded from training data, and both fighters need at least two prior UFC fights — plus 7 draws/no-contests. Betting uses the 281 scored fights with usable closing odds, de-vigged to remove the bookmaker's margin.

Calibration

Confidence still means something — it's just shy

For Kelly sizing the question isn't "how often is it right," it's "when it says 70%, does it win 70%?" The answer this year: more than it claims. Every confidence band landed at or above its stated probability, rising from 60% on coin-flip calls to 81.8% on its most confident picks. The one wobble is the 60–65 band (62.9% on 62 fights), inside noise.

On the 281 fights with odds, the model and the market favor the same fighter 212 times (75%) — and those picks hit 74.1%. On the 69 fights where they disagree, the model wins only 46.4% to the market's 53.6%. Head-to-head as a predictor, the market is simply sharper: it prices injuries, camp changes, weigh-ins and late news that a career-stats model never sees.

That sounds fatal for betting. It isn't — because a bettor doesn't need to out-predict the market, only to find prices that pay more than a well-calibrated probability says they should. That's the next section.

To separate the model's edge from Kelly compounding, the same year at a flat $10 per fight:

  • Always bet the market favorite: +0.8% per bet — the market is accurate but pays you nothing for agreeing with it.
  • Always bet the model's pick: +8.1% per bet — same fights, same prices, meaningfully positive.
  • Bet the "value side" wherever model and market differ by ≥5 points: −3.5% per bet — chasing raw disagreement loses.

Analysis

Why this works — and where it doesn't

  • The edge is price selection, not prophecy. The model predicts slightly worse than the market, yet its picks returned +8.1% flat where favorites returned +0.8%. The profit comes from which agreements it sizes up: fights where its calibrated probability says the price is soft. Kelly then compounds a small per-bet edge into +13% to +21% for the year.
  • Raw disagreement is a warning sign, not a signal. When the model and market split, the model wins 46.4% — and mechanically betting the model's side of every ≥5-point gap lost 3.5% per bet. Large gaps usually mean the market knows something the model structurally can't: injuries, short-notice replacements, camp and weight-cut news. The production configs survive this because positive-Kelly filtering and the market blend keep stakes small where the model is most likely blind.
  • Calibration is the asset to protect. Accuracy alone barely separates a good year from a bad one (67% vs 69% for the market). What made betting work is that stated confidence tracked reality band by band — the property the 2024–25 stale-model era lost first. The twice-yearly retrain cadence held it this year.
  • Legacy beat the deployed blend this year — don't act on it. +21.5% vs +13.3% is one year and ~200 Kelly bets of separation; over the 2.5-year 2024–26 study the blend won. The blend's lower drawdown (7.5% vs 8.6%) and its protection on disagreement fights are structural; the single-year gap is noise until it repeats.
  • Honest error bars. 67.0% accuracy on 282 fights carries a ±2.8-point standard error; every ROI figure rides on under 200 bets and their sequencing. Treat the sign as meaningful, not the second digit.
  • Coverage is the ceiling. The model can only act on 52% of fights: no women's bouts (excluded upstream in cleaning), no debutants, nobody with fewer than two UFC fights. The largest untapped improvement in this report isn't a better model — it's a wider one.

  • Underdog bets carry the largest stakes and the least edge. Kelly sizes by model-vs-price divergence, and the model diverges most on underdogs — exactly where it's most often blind. Result: the biggest stakes (≈2× the favorite bets) ride 42%-hit outcomes. That's the bleed-then-spike: frequent small losses, occasional +330 paydays. Over 2.5 years dogs netted roughly zero (legacy) or −$73 (blend); in the last year alone they made +$110 — a lucky season, not a dependable engine.
  • Over half of all bets are forced. A legacy fallback stakes 0.5% flat even when Kelly is negative — 278–296 of ~520 bets, netting ≈ zero. It survives in betting_alpha.py (production recommendations); predict_event.py already refuses these bets.

  • The underdog lever moves the frontier; the others fine-tune it. Every low-drawdown, solid-return point is a half-stake or skip-dogs config. Best of the grid on the full window: legacy anchor + 4-pt edge gate + half-stake dogs → +15.0% at 7.7% drawdown, and it repeats last year (+13.0% at 4.6%). Skipping dogs entirely is the smoothest ride (≈+14% at 3–4% drawdown) at the cost of last year's dog windfall.
  • Dropping forced bets costs ≈0–1 pt of return and removes half the betting volume. It's the cheapest cleanup, and principled: the strategy shouldn't stake picks its own math scores negative.
  • The 3% fraction is strictly a comfort dial. It scales return and drawdown down together without fixing the composition problem the dog policy fixes.
  • Selection caveat, stated plainly: these 48 cells were both diagnosed and ranked on the same two report-only windows. The forced-bet removal stands on principle; the dog-policy change is a strong hypothesis that should be confirmed on a window that didn't generate it (2021–23) or paper-traded a cycle before real money moves.
